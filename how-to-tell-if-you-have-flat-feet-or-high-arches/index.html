How to Tell If You Have Flat Feet or High Arches: The 3-Step Wet Paper Footprint Test

By Elena Rostova (Podiatry & Skincare Specialist) • November 1, 2026 • 9 min read
Direct Answer & Medical Summary

To determine whether you have flat feet, normal arches, or high arches, perform the 3-step Wet Paper Footprint Test. Wet the bottom of your bare foot in shallow water, step firmly onto a piece of dark cardboard or brown craft paper, and inspect the imprint. A complete, solid footprint with no inward curve indicates flat feet (pes planus); a footprint showing half the arch width indicates normal arches; a thin outer strip or detached heel and forefoot indicates high arches (pes cavus).

Key Clinical Takeaways & Rules
  • The Wet Paper Test provides 90% diagnostic correlation with podiatric static arch assessments.
  • Flat feet (pes planus) display a solid footprint with zero arch indentation.
  • High arches (pes cavus) show only a narrow outer line connecting the ball and heel.
  • Check shoe wear patterns: inner heel wear indicates flat feet; outer tread wear indicates high arches.
  • The Navicular Drop Test measures whether your arch sinks more than 10 mm under weight-bearing.

1. Biological Mechanism & Tissue Dynamics

The human foot bears up to 1.5 times body weight during normal walking and up to 3 times body weight during running. The epidermal stratum corneum on the soles of our feet contains up to 50 compacted layers of dead, anucleate corneocyte cells bound by specialized lipid envelopes (ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids).

When shear stress, unyielding footwear friction, or abnormal gait mechanics overload specific pressure points, basal keratinocytes accelerate the production of cytokeratins. Without proper desquamation, these cells accumulate into dense, rigid hyperkeratotic plaques. Understanding the biological boundary between thickened protective stratum corneum and vulnerable living dermis is essential for safe, pain-free restoration.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can flat feet become normal arches with exercises?

In adults with flexible flat feet, targeted intrinsic foot exercises (such as towel scrunches and short-foot toe curls) can strengthen the posterior tibial muscle and plantar fascia, improving dynamic arch stability, but cannot permanently remodel adult osseous bone structure.

Are high arches worse than flat feet?

Both present unique biomechanical challenges. While flat feet suffer from excessive joint instability and overpronation strain, high arches are rigid and inflexible, absorbing ground shock poorly and transferring excessive impact into the knees, hips, and lower spine.

Why does my arch hurt if I buy an insole that is too high?

If you have low or flexible flat arches and place a rigid high-arch insole inside your shoe, the hard peak of the orthotic will dig directly into your navicular bone and plantar nerve bundle, causing bruising and severe cramping.

How does footwear wear pattern confirm your arch type?

Examine the bottom treads of an old pair of running or walking shoes. If the rubber is worn down smooth along the inside edge near the big toe, you overpronate (flat feet). If worn along the outside outer rim, you supinate (high arches).
