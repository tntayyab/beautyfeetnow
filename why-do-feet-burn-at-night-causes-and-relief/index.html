Why Do Feet Burn at Night? Grierson-Gopalan Syndrome Causes & Relief Strategies

By Elena Rostova (Podiatry Specialist) • November 2, 2026 • 11 min read
Direct Answer & Medical Summary

Feet burn at night primarily due to small-fiber peripheral neuropathy, where damaged sensory nerve fibers fire spontaneous pain signals into the brain during rest. Known clinically as Grierson-Gopalan syndrome, nocturnal exacerbation occurs because evening cortisol levels drop, distraction diminishes, and peripheral vascular pooling occurs when lying supine. Contributing factors include Vitamin B12 deficiency, diabetes, alcohol toxicity, hypothyroidism, and chronic tarsal tunnel compression.

Key Clinical Takeaways & Rules
  • Small-fiber sensory neuropathy accounts for 70% of nocturnal burning feet complaints.
  • Evening circadian drops in anti-inflammatory cortisol intensify nerve pain perception.
  • Vitamin B12 deficiency below 300 pg/mL impairs peripheral myelin sheath repair.
  • Pneumatic air massagers and cool (not freezing) foot baths calm nocturnal parasthesia.
  • Always consult a physician to screen for HbA1c (diabetes) and thyroid markers.

1. Biological Mechanism & Tissue Dynamics

The human foot bears up to 1.5 times body weight during normal walking and up to 3 times body weight during running. The epidermal stratum corneum on the soles of our feet contains up to 50 compacted layers of dead, anucleate corneocyte cells bound by specialized lipid envelopes (ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids).

When shear stress, unyielding footwear friction, or abnormal gait mechanics overload specific pressure points, basal keratinocytes accelerate the production of cytokeratins. Without proper desquamation, these cells accumulate into dense, rigid hyperkeratotic plaques. Understanding the biological boundary between thickened protective stratum corneum and vulnerable living dermis is essential for safe, pain-free restoration.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Should you put ice on burning feet?

No! Never plunge burning feet directly into ice water or ice packs. If the underlying cause is diabetic neuropathy or compromised circulation, cold-induced vasoconstriction can cause severe frostbite damage and tissue necrosis without you feeling the extreme cold.

Can poor foot circulation cause burning feet at night?

Yes. Paradoxically, poor peripheral vascular microcirculation leads to tissue hypoxia in the nerves of the feet. Deprived of oxygen, nerve endings fire irregular electrical distress impulses that the brain registers as intense burning heat.

What vitamins help with burning feet?

Vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin), Vitamin B1 (benfotiamine), Vitamin B6, and Alpha-Lipoic Acid (ALA at 600 mg daily) are clinically verified to support myelin nerve sheath repair and mitigate neuropathic burning sensations.

Can tight shoes during the day cause feet to burn at night?

Yes. Tight, restrictive footwear compresses the medial and lateral plantar nerves against the tarsal tunnel throughout the day. When shoes are removed at night, reperfusion and decompression of the irritated nerves trigger throbbing, burning sensations.
